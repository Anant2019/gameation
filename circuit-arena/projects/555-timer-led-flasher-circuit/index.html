The 555 is the most famous chip in electronics. In astable mode it switches its output on and off by itself, so an LED blinks with no code at all.

How it works

  1. The 555 charges and discharges a capacitor through resistors.
  2. Each time the capacitor crosses a threshold, the output flips between high and low.
  3. The output drives the LED through a resistor, so the LED blinks.

Code

This circuit needs no code: the chips do all the work.

Tips for building the real thing

  • Blink rate ≈ 1.44 / ((R1 + 2·R2) · C). Bigger resistors or capacitor = slower blink.
  • Pin 4 (RESET) must be tied to + or the chip will not run.

FAQ

What are the modes of a 555 timer?

Astable (free-running, like this flasher), monostable (one pulse) and bistable (flip-flop).

What voltage does a 555 need?

4.5 V to 15 V for the common NE555.
